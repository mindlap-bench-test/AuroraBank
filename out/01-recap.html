Aurora Bank — Discovery Recap

What we heard in the discovery call, played back in your words.

Discovery draft · illustrative · to validate with Aurora Bank.

The problem

"The website is a maze. If you want to compare FD rates, book one, and also look at a credit card, that's — four, five different sections?" — Rohan Desai, Head of Digital Channels

Customers navigate a fragmented site — some content behind login, some not — to do things that feel like they should be one conversation. Credit card applications lose people mid-form; fixed deposit booking is "leaky" though less so. The call center absorbs volume that should be self-serve: FD maturity dates, application status, KYC status.

Objectives / what success looks like

  • Lift the credit card application completion rate — a lot of people start and don't finish.
  • Cut product discovery time — how fast someone goes from "I have money" to "I booked an FD."
  • Reduce the number of pages/sections a customer has to navigate — ideally close to zero.
  • Deflect self-serve questions away from the call center (maturity dates, application status, KYC status).
  • Zero compliance incidents — "a wrong balance shown to the wrong person is worse than any conversion gain." — Sana Qureshi, Compliance & Risk

Core use-cases

FD Compare-and-Book
Prospective & existing customers — compare FD tenures/rates, get a recommendation, and book in one conversation.
HERO
Card Recommend-and-Apply
Prospective & existing customers — a few questions on spend habits, a card recommendation (Aurora Voyage / Everyday / Infinia), then a conversational application.
HERO
Loan eligibility & indicative rate
"Am I likely to qualify, and at what rate" — collect basics, hand off to start the application. assume: personal loan only in v1 — scope undecided
Basic servicing
Existing, logged-in customers — balance inquiry, track an application, proactive re-KYC reminders.
Mid-journey rescue
Recognize a customer who started something (an FD booking, a card application) and didn't finish, and offer to pick it back up.

Constraints & context

ThingWhat they said
Scale~3.8 million customers, mostly individuals and small businesses.
Products in scopeSavings/current accounts, fixed deposits, personal/home/car loans, credit cards (Aurora Voyage, Aurora Everyday, Aurora Infinia).
Core bankingCorvus — system of record for accounts, deposits, transactions. FD booking ultimately lands here.
CRM / servicingSalesforce — customer 360, used by the call center; also hosts live chat agents.
Legacy depositsLedgerOne — an AS/400-era ledger; a chunk of older FDs haven't migrated to Corvus, so "show me all my FDs" spans two systems.
RatesAn internal, versioned Rates Service API (treasury team) for FD and loan indicative rates.
Card applicationsFlow through a separate card origination system.
AuthNetBanking login; OTP step-up required for sensitive, money-moving actions (e.g. booking an FD) even inside an authenticated session.
KYCCKYC (centralized registry) plus a video-KYC flow for new customers. The assistant may collect info and check status, but cannot complete KYC itself.
ComplianceEvery interaction touching customer data must be audit-logged. Fair-lending rules govern how loans are discussed; marketing/legal review the recommendation logic.

Success metrics

  • Card application completion rate — from ~40% completing today to a target of ~60%.
  • Product discovery time — time from "I have money" to "I booked an FD." assume: no baseline/target number given yet
  • Pages/sections navigated per task — ideally close to zero. assume: no baseline number given yet
  • Call center deflection — fewer self-serve calls (maturity dates, application status, KYC status).
  • Zero compliance incidents — no account-specific data shown to the wrong person.

Open questions & assumptions to validate

  • assume Whether the assistant submits an FD booking directly into Corvus, or hands off to the existing booking form at the last step — an open architectural fork, not yet decided.
  • assume Which loan types belong in v1 — personal loan eligibility is the safer self-serve starting point; home and car loans have a heavier offline/relationship-manager component. Not decided.
  • assume Whether the Rates Service is real-time or batch — the team believes FD rates update intraday and loan rates are a morning batch, but this needs confirming.
  • assume Where KYC status truth lives — Corvus and Salesforce both hold it and don't always agree; source of truth is unresolved.
  • assume Language support beyond English (Hindi and regional languages) — raised as a real need but not yet scoped. Parked.
  • assume Human handoff design — live chat agents exist in Salesforce and a handoff should ideally carry context, but this hasn't been designed. Parked.
